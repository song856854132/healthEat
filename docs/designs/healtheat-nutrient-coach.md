## Problem Statement

Calorie trackers end at a number. People who eat "healthy" and stay under their calorie target can still be short on nutrients that keep energy and performance up, and can still be inflamed by an ingredient their body reacts to. In the user's words: "even eating healthy food without over-consuming calories can inflame the body via an allergen."

healthEat is a web app that behaves like a coach rather than a diary. It tells the user when the week is running low on a nutrient and which real foods close the gap, flags any trace of a true allergen the moment it is logged, and tracks cumulative exposure to sensitivities over the week. It must also satisfy the Kambaz open-ended final project rubric at the graduate tier.

User zero is the builder (own allergy or nutrient concern). Secondary audiences: a friend or family member with an allergy, and the grader. There is no human coach in the product: the platform is the coach.

## What Makes This Cool

- **Weekly coach, not daily meter.** Nutrient coverage and sensitivity exposure render as rolling 7-day bands with a confidence marker per day. Dietitians assess intake over a week; no competitor found does. Photo-estimate error (NIH testing: 250 to 345 kcal under per meal) becomes noise that averages out instead of a number the user distrusts.
- **Hidden-allergen detection you own.** USDA branded foods carry ingredient text. A Curator-editable synonym dictionary ("soy lecithin", "whey", "casein") matches it. Any hit on a true allergy is a hard alert; hits on sensitivities accumulate into a weekly meter with user-set thresholds.
- **Photo as input, USDA as truth.** A meal photo goes to Claude with a structured-output schema and comes back as a list of foods with gram estimates and confidence. The user confirms or edits. Every nutrient value then comes from USDA FoodData Central, never from the model's memory.
- **The platform is the coach.** No human coach role. The bands, the "close the gap" foods, the hard alerts and the exposure meter are the coaching, computed from the member's own logs. A Curator role keeps the dictionary and the nutrient targets honest; an Admin role keeps accounts in order. Neither role can read any member's logs.
- **Symptom overlay (phase 3).** One-tap symptom logging (headache, bloating, skin, energy 1 to 5) plotted on the same axis as exposure bands with a 12 to 24 hour lag. This is the elimination diet as software, framed as "patterns to discuss with a clinician", never diagnosis.

## Premises

1. Weekly coach, not daily meter. 7-day rolling bands with confidence for nutrients and sensitivities. A coarse daily calorie meter (under, on target, over) stays as a reminder with no precision claim. User's words: "every single day meter serves as a calorie reminder for user, no need for precision."
2. Two tiers of "allergen". True allergies: binary avoid list, hard alert on any detected trace. Sensitivities and intolerances (lactose, gluten, soy, histamine-rich foods): cumulative exposure meter, counted as ingredient keyword hits weighted by grams, labelled as such because histamine and gluten are not USDA nutrients. Visible "not medical advice" notice.
3. Photo is an input method, not the data source. Claude vision proposes items and grams; the user confirms; USDA supplies nutrients. Portions are always editable.
4. The allergen dictionary is owned code and a first-class domain model, editable by the Curator role (never by Admin, whose job is accounts). Seeded from the Open Food Facts allergens taxonomy (CC BY-SA, attribution in the About and Privacy pages).
5. Rubric is the floor. Six screens, three roles (Member, Curator, Admin), USDA remote API, Mongo model clearing graduate minimums, UML Design wiki. Everything beyond the floor is phased.
6. (Revised after the second opinion.) The API host is an explicit architecture decision, not a footnote. Chosen: Express behind a same-origin proxy (Approach C below).

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Next.js Monolith
One Next.js app on Vercel; API in route handlers. Effort S, risk Medium. Fastest and simplest, same-origin cookies. Rejected because it bets the grade on route handlers counting as "Express", and the fix if wrong (a cross-origin split) rewrites auth under deadline.

### Approach B: Course-Conventional Split
Next.js on Vercel plus a separate Express API on Render or Railway, cross-origin. Effort M-L, risk Medium. Literal match to the rubric and course shape; a long-lived server could run scheduled jobs. Rejected because two origins mean CORS with credentials, SameSite=None cookies, preview URLs that break login, and a free-tier server that sleeps (about 50 s cold start) during demos.

### Approach C: Same-Origin Proxy (chosen)
Monorepo with npm workspaces. `apps/web` is Next.js, `apps/api` is Express, `packages/shared` holds TypeScript types, the allergen dictionary seed, and DRI tables. Next.js `rewrites` proxies `/api/*` to the Express service, so the browser sees one origin: plain cookies, no CORS. Express deploys as its own Vercel project (Vercel has a native Express preset) or anywhere else by changing one rewrite URL. Effort M, risk Low to Medium.

## Recommended Approach

Approach C. Express satisfies the rubric literally; the proxy removes every cross-origin cost; and hosting is reversible in both directions (collapse into A if the instructor approves route handlers, or point the proxy at Render if they want a visibly separate server). The half-day monorepo tax is the price of not betting the semester on one email.

Wireframe of the three Home screens (Member, Curator, Admin): `docs/designs/healtheat-home-wireframe.html` in the repo. Hierarchy on Member Home: hard allergy alert strip (only when triggered), today's coarse calorie meter, 7-day nutrient band (hero), sensitivity exposure meter, "close the gap" foods, persistent log bar with search and photo.

### Data model (Mongoose)

User models (3). Accounts are multi-role: one `User` document plus one profile document per role the account holds, in separate collections keyed by `userId`. No discriminator, so a curator who also logs their own meals has both a `CuratorProfile` and a `MemberProfile`.

- `User`: `username` (immutable, unique), `email`, `passwordHash` (bcrypt), `roles: ("member"|"curator"|"admin")[]` (every account has `member`; `curator` and `admin` granted by Admin), `status` ("active"|"suspended"; suspended accounts cannot log in), `activeRole` (which Home and nav render; persisted here and mirrored in the session), `privacyAckAt?`, `createdAt`.
- `MemberProfile` (userId unique): `sex`, `birthYear`, `heightCm`, `weightKg`, `activityLevel`, `calorieBand {low, high}`, `driSetKey` (resolves to a DRI row), `timezone` (IANA, defaulted from the browser's `Intl.DateTimeFormat().resolvedOptions().timeZone` at registration, editable), `publicLogs: false` (opt-in to show username on Details), `showPhoneOnProfile: false`, `visionUsage: {date, count}`.
- `CuratorProfile` (userId unique): `credentials?` (free text shown on the public profile), `bio`, `specialties[]` (for example "dairy", "FODMAP"), `publicListing: false` (opt in to the anonymous Home feed), `editsCount`, `lastEditAt`. Distinct attributes: `editsCount`, `lastEditAt`.
- `AdminProfile` (userId unique): `lastAuditAt`, `grantsCount` (role changes made), `internalNote?`. Distinct attributes: `lastAuditAt`, `grantsCount`.

Role rules: `requireRole(r)` middleware passes when `user.roles.includes(r)`, independent of `activeRole`. `activeRole` only selects which Home renders and which nav items show; `PUT /api/users/me/active-role` changes it (must be in `roles`). Profile edits target the profile by kind: `PUT /api/users/me/profiles/:kind` where `kind` is `member`, `curator`, or `admin` and the caller holds that role.

Domain models (5; 3 in phase 0):

- `Food` (cache of USDA items the users have touched): `fdcId` (unique), `description`, `dataType`, `brandOwner?`, `ingredientsText?`, `nutrients: [{nutrientId, name, unit, amountPer100g}]`, `servingSizes[]`, `fetchedAt`. TTL re-fetch after 30 days.
- `LogEntry`: `userId`, `date` (YYYY-MM-DD in the member's `timezone`; taken from the request's optional `date` field when supplied, otherwise computed server-side from the request's UTC timestamp; "today" everywhere means the current date in that timezone), `meal` (breakfast/lunch/dinner/snack), `fdcId`, `grams`, `source` ("search"|"photo"), `confidence` (0 to 1; 1 for search), `photoBatchId?`, `createdAt`.
- `Allergen`: `key`, `displayName`, `tier` ("allergy"|"sensitivity"|"both"), `synonyms: string[]` (lowercased ingredient tokens and phrases), `descriptionKeywords: string[]` (D12), `source` ("off-taxonomy"|"curator"), `updatedBy` (curator userId; the Curator-to-Allergen one-to-many), `rematchCursor?` (fdcId of the last cached Food re-matched after the latest synonym edit; null when complete).
- `SymptomEntry` (phase 3): `userId`, `at` (datetime), `symptom` (enum), `severity` (1 to 5), `note?`.
- `IngredientToken` (phase 3): `token` (normalised ingredient phrase), `count` (distinct cached Foods carrying it), `sampleFdcIds[]` (up to 5), `status` ("new"|"mapped"|"ignored"), `reviewedBy?` (curator userId), `reviewedAt?`. Upserted once per Food at cache time for each phrase that matched no synonym; the Curator queue sorts by `count`. Mapping a token appends it to an Allergen's synonyms and runs that Allergen's re-match.

Join collections for the many-to-many relationships:

- `UserAllergen`: `userId`, `allergenId`, `mode` ("avoid"|"track"), `weeklyThreshold?` (hits per 7 days, for track mode). Unique on (userId, allergenId).
- `FoodAllergen`: `fdcId`, `allergenId`, `matchedSynonym`, `matchedAt`. Written when a Food is cached or the dictionary changes (re-match job). Unique on (fdcId, allergenId).
- `SymptomSuspect` (phase 3): `symptomEntryId`, `allergenId`, `lagHours`, `score`.

Rubric check: one-to-many = User to LogEntry, Curator to Allergen (`updatedBy`), User to SymptomEntry (phase 3), Curator to IngredientToken (`reviewedBy`, phase 3) (need 2, have 2 in phase 0 and 4 by phase 3). Many-to-many = User to Allergen (UserAllergen), Food to Allergen (FoodAllergen) (need 2, have 2 in phase 0; SymptomEntry to Allergen via SymptomSuspect adds a third in phase 3). Domain models: Food, LogEntry, Allergen in phase 0, plus SymptomEntry and IngredientToken in phase 3 (need 3). User models: MemberProfile, CuratorProfile, AdminProfile, each with distinct attributes (need 3).

UML "Design" wiki page lists at minimum: User, MemberProfile, CuratorProfile, AdminProfile, Food, LogEntry, Allergen, UserAllergen, FoodAllergen, SymptomEntry.

### Core algorithms

**Daily totals.** For each LogEntry on a date: `amount = nutrient.amountPer100g * grams / 100` per nutrient. Sum per nutrient per day. Confidence for the day = grams logged from search / total grams (so a day that is 70% photo-estimated shows a hollow dot).

**7-day coverage.** For window W of the 7 dates ending `weekEnding`, let L be the subset of W with at least one LogEntry. `coverage(n) = sum_{d in L} intake(n,d) / (|L| * target(n))`, where `target` comes from the DRI table row for the member's sex and age band (RDA or AI; for sodium use the CDRR as the "high" line). Days with no logs are excluded from both numerator and denominator, so a partial week is not uniformly "low"; instead the band header says "4 of 7 days logged" and the row's confidence indicator scales with `|L| / 7`. With `|L| = 0` the band renders empty with "no logs yet". Band classes: below 0.70 low, 0.70 to 1.30 ok, above 1.30 high (upper lines use UL where defined). Demo check: on a single logged day with vitamin E at 41% of a 15 mg RDA, adding 28 g sunflower seeds (7.4 mg, 49%) moves the row to 90%, which is "ok".

**Today's calorie meter.** Sum of today's kcal against the member's `calorieBand`; rendered as under, on target, over. Band default from Mifflin-St Jeor times activity factor, plus or minus 10%, editable.

**Allergen matching.** Normalise `ingredientsText` (lowercase, strip punctuation, split on commas and parentheses). For each Allergen, for each synonym, match as a whole-word or phrase. Write FoodAllergen rows. Foundation and SR Legacy foods have no ingredient text: match on the description only against a small curated list (for example "soy", "milk", "wheat").

**Re-match on dictionary change.** Serverless functions freeze after the response, so there is no background pass. A dictionary write handles the re-match inside the same request: it sets `rematchCursor` to the first cached fdcId, then walks the `Food` collection in batches of 500 ordered by `fdcId`, re-running the matcher for that one Allergen and replacing its FoodAllergen rows, advancing the cursor after each batch. Matching is in-memory string work; 500 foods take well under a second. The cache is expected to hold at most a few thousand Foods in a semester (only foods users touched), so the pass completes in seconds. The whole Express API is one Vercel function with a 300 s window (the Hobby default and maximum; nothing to configure). As a guard the handler stops after 60 seconds, well under that limit, leaves `rematchCursor` set, and returns 202 with `{done:false, processed, remaining}`; the Curator dictionary page then shows "Re-matching… N remaining" and calls `POST /api/curate/allergens/:id/rematch` until `done:true`. Independently of the 202, the Curator dictionary page shows a "Continue re-match" action on any Allergen whose `rematchCursor` is non-null, so a request lost to a timeout or a closed tab is recoverable. The Curator sees a "Re-matched N foods" confirmation on completion.

**Exposure meter.** For each tracked allergen: `hits = count of LogEntry in W whose Food has a FoodAllergen row`, weighted `sum(grams)/100` as a secondary figure. Compare `hits` against `weeklyThreshold`. Hard-alert path: any FoodAllergen row on today's log for an avoid-mode allergen.

**Close the gap.** Candidate set = cached Foods plus a curated seed list of about 60 whole foods with fdcIds. Score = `amountPer100g(n) / kcalPer100g`, drop any Food with a FoodAllergen row for an avoid-mode allergen, return the top 6 with a realistic portion.

**Photo pipeline.** Browser downsizes to max 1024 px JPEG (canvas), typically under 250 KB, well below Vercel's 4.5 MB cap and 10x cheaper in image tokens. `POST /api/vision/meal` sends the image to the Anthropic SDK, model `claude-opus-5-5`, with `output_config.format` set to a JSON schema `{items: [{query: string, grams: number, confidence: number}]}` and a system prompt that forbids nutrient estimates. Cost is about one cent per photo. Response items are each resolved with a USDA search; the user sees the matches and grams, edits, and confirms; only then are LogEntry rows written with `source: "photo"`. Server-side fallback to a sibling model is enabled on refusal. The vision call runs inside the single API function's 300 s window (see Re-match on dictionary change); the Anthropic client timeout is set to 60 s so a stuck call fails fast with a retryable error.

### Auth and sessions

`express-session` with `connect-mongo` store (rubric: "maintain session", and sessions survive serverless cold starts). Cookie `httpOnly`, `secure` in production, `sameSite: "lax"`. Because the browser only ever talks to the Next.js origin, no CORS configuration is needed. Password hashed with bcrypt (cost 12). Role guard middleware `requireRole("curator")` and `requireRole("admin")`; member data routes are `me`-scoped, so no account can address another member's logs. Username is immutable at the model and route level. Passwords never returned; the profile edit form has a "show password" toggle on the input only.

### Deployment

- Vercel project `healtheat-web` (built from `apps/web`), env: `API_ORIGIN` (preview: the staging API alias; production: `https://healtheat-api.vercel.app`).
- Vercel project `healtheat-api` (built from `apps/api`, Express preset via `vercel.json`), env: `MONGODB_URI`, `MONGODB_DB` (required on every deployment; preview, staging and production never fall back to the dev database), `SESSION_SECRET` (a deployment still on the local placeholder answers 503 `secret_unconfigured` on every route except the `/api/health` routes, `/api/health` and `/api/health/db`), `USDA_API_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`. Keys never reach the browser. `GET /api/health` reports `db`, `dbName` and `secrets` without forcing a connection; `GET /api/health/db` forces one and answers 503 unless it is connected (the staging and release smokes use it). Contract: `apps/api/README.md`.
- MongoDB Atlas M0 (512 MB): one cluster for every environment (decided 2026-11-11 when the cluster was created); `MONGODB_URI` is the cluster URI without a database name and `MONGODB_DB` selects `healtheat_prod`, `healtheat_staging`, `healtheat_preview` or `healtheat_dev_<user>`. Mongoose connection cached on `globalThis` for warm invocations, `maxPoolSize 5`, `serverSelectionTimeoutMS 5000`.
- GitHub Actions is the only deployer: `ci` on every push and PR; `preview` deploys both apps per PR and runs Playwright through the proxy; `staging` deploys `main` and aliases it to `healtheat-staging.vercel.app` / `healtheat-api-staging.vercel.app`; `release` deploys production on a `v*` tag from `main`. Cookies are same-origin on every preview URL. See `docs/team-workflow.md`.
- USDA key: 1000 requests per hour per IP by default. Cache-on-read (`Food`) plus the `SearchCache` collection (24 h TTL, D5) keeps a grader's clicking far below that; on a USDA 429 or 5xx the cached page is served with a "cached" label.
